# Deoxyribonucleic acid

The DNA double helix is a molecular structure consisting of two complementary strands of DNA that twist around each other.

![[dna-helix.png]]

DNA is short for deoxyribonucleic acid.

## Mutation of DNA

A genetic mutation is a change in the nucleotide sequence of an organism’s DNA.

## Repeair

Without effective DNA repair mechanisms, damage to the genetic material would remain unrepaired, potentially leading to the progressive accumulation of mutations, impaired cellular functions, genomic instability, and ultimately severe consequences for cell survival, organismal health, and the maintenance of genetic integrity across generations.

# DNA Instability: Types and Causes

1. **Depurination:** The spontaneous loss of a purine base (adenine or guanine) from DNA, leaving an abasic site.

2. **Deamination:** A chemical reaction that removes an amino group from a DNA base, potentially altering its identity and causing mutations.

3. **DNA Strand Breaks:** Damage to the sugar-phosphate backbone, resulting in single-strand or double-strand breaks.

4. **Oxidative Damage:** Chemical modifications to DNA caused by reactive oxygen species, potentially affecting its structure and genetic information.

5. **Hydrolytic Damage:** Chemical deterioration caused by reactions with water, including the breakdown of bonds within DNA.

6. **Thermal Instability:** Elevated temperatures accelerate chemical degradation and can cause the two strands of the DNA double helix to separate.

7. **UV-Induced Damage:** Ultraviolet radiation can create abnormal bonds between neighboring DNA bases, distorting the double helix.

8. **Ionizing Radiation Damage:** High-energy radiation can damage DNA bases and cause single-strand or double-strand breaks.

9. **Replication Errors:** Incorrect nucleotide incorporation during DNA replication can produce mutations if the errors are not corrected.

10. **Chemical Instability:** Exposure to certain chemicals can modify DNA bases, create cross-links, or damage the DNA backbone.

DNA is not chemically indestructible. Without effective repair mechanisms, damage can accumulate over time, compromising genetic integrity and biological functionality.

DNA is chemically destructible.
